What this Image to PDF Converter does

An image-to-PDF workflow takes picture files such as JPG, PNG, or WebP and places them onto PDF pages. The PDF format is useful because it keeps pages in a fixed layout, which makes it suitable for receipts, assignments, scanned forms, photo collections, presentations, and document archives. This converter gives you control over page order, page orientation, margins, filters, watermarks, and image quality before you export.

The main benefit is convenience. You can upload multiple images, review them as pages, remove anything unwanted, rotate a sideways scan, apply a grayscale filter to a mixed batch, and then download a single document. The tool is focused on image-to-PDF conversion. It does not perform OCR or create editable text.

How to convert images to PDF

  1. Add your images. Use the upload area at the top of this page — click it or drag files onto it. JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF and BMP are supported.
  2. Review the page grid. Check that every image appears in the order you want. Drag any card to reorder — or use the sort and reverse buttons in the toolbar.
  3. Fix orientation and unwanted pages. Rotate individual pages left or right in 90° steps. Duplicate a page when needed. Remove pages with a single click.
  4. Choose layout settings. Pick A4, Letter, Legal, Fit to Image, or a custom size. Then choose Portrait or Landscape, and set the margins.
  5. Apply filters and finishing touches. Use the image filter buttons for grayscale, sepia, brightness or contrast. Open Advanced options to add a watermark, header, footer and page numbers.
  6. Adjust quality. Use a preset or the precision slider to balance file size and sharpness.
  7. Convert and download. Press Convert to PDF. When it finishes you can preview the file, download it, share it, print it, or start over.

Privacy note: every image is processed locally in your browser using the Canvas API. Files are never uploaded. For extra confidence, disconnect from the internet after loading the page — conversion still works.

Supported image formats and practical tips

The uploader accepts JPG, JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF, and BMP — plus any other format your browser can decode. JPG files are often smaller and work well for photos. PNG files are useful for screenshots, diagrams, and images with transparency, though they tend to be larger. WebP files are common on the web and offer strong compression, but some older workflows may not open them as easily.

For the best results, use clear source images. A sharp, well-lit photo will usually produce a more readable PDF than a blurry or heavily compressed one. If you are converting text-heavy scans, make sure the text is straight and large enough to read. If an image is extremely large, consider resizing it before upload if the tool slows down or the output file becomes too heavy.

Page management before export

Page management is one of the most useful parts of an image-to-PDF workflow. A batch of photos rarely arrives in perfect order, and scans often need small corrections. Use the toolbar to sort by name, reverse the order, or drag any card to move it. Hover any page to rotate it left or right, duplicate it, or remove it. Select multiple pages with the checkboxes and use the batch delete button to fix them all at once.

Review the page grid carefully before downloading. Check for missing pages, accidental duplicates, and pages that are rotated the wrong way. If you are creating a document for printing, confirm the first page is the cover or first page you expect. If you are assembling a photo collection, keep related images together so the final PDF reads naturally.

Page size, orientation, margins, and scaling

Layout settings affect how each image sits on the PDF page. Common page sizes include A4, Letter, Legal, and Fit to Image. A4 and Letter are standard document sizes for many offices and schools. Legal is longer and may suit certain forms or contracts. Fit to Image is useful when you want the PDF page to follow the image dimensions rather than forcing a standard paper size. Custom size is there when you need an exact width and height in millimetres.

Orientation matters too. Portrait is usually best for documents, letters, and vertical photos. Landscape works better for wide screenshots, spreadsheets, panoramic photos, and presentation slides. Margins can be set to None, Small, Medium, or Large. No margins may look clean for full-page images, but small or medium margins make printing safer because they reduce the risk of content being cut off by a printer's non-printable area.

Images are fitted proportionally inside the printable area and centred on the page, so nothing is stretched or cropped. Choose the page size that matches your goal: a consistent document layout, or a page that follows each image's own shape.

Quality, compression, and export options

Image quality and compression settings help balance file size and sharpness. Higher quality means a larger PDF with clearer details, which is better for printing, signatures, receipts, and text-heavy scans. Stronger compression can produce a smaller file that is easier to email or upload, but fine details may become softer. Use the quality presets as a starting point, or slide the precision control to dial in exactly what you need.

A custom output filename makes the final PDF easier to find later. Use a short, descriptive name such as "receipts-march" or "assignment-pages". You can also set PDF metadata (title and author) from the Advanced options panel. After export, confirm the download started correctly and that the PDF opens in your usual viewer. Some browsers may block automatic downloads — if so, check your browser prompts and allow the site.

Privacy and browser-based performance

This converter processes images locally in your browser using client-side conversion, which means files are not uploaded to an external server. This is helpful for privacy because the image data stays on your device during conversion. Actual behaviour depends on the live page implementation, so confirm the current privacy behaviour before converting confidential documents, identity papers, or private photos.

Browser-based processing also means performance depends on your device. Large batches, high-resolution scans, and many pages can use significant memory. If the page becomes slow, try converting fewer images at a time, closing unused browser tabs, or reducing very large image files before upload.

Scanning and print best practices

Good source images make better PDFs. When scanning documents, keep pages straight, avoid shadows, and use enough resolution to make small text readable. For photos, use even lighting and avoid excessive digital zoom, which can make the image blurry. If you are photographing a document with a phone, hold the camera parallel to the page and keep the entire page inside the frame.

For printing, choose a page size that matches your paper and leave a small margin if your printer cannot print edge to edge. Preview the PDF before printing a large job. If the document will be shared online, moderate compression is often enough; if it will be printed or archived, prioritise clarity over the smallest possible file size.

Troubleshooting common problems

An image will not upload

Check that the file is a supported image format and that it is not corrupted. Try opening the image in another app first. If the format is unusual, convert it to JPG or PNG and upload again. Very large files can also be rejected by the browser — try a smaller version.

The page is slow or the browser runs out of memory

Convert fewer images per batch, close other tabs, and avoid extremely large scans. If you have many pages, split the job into two or three PDFs.

The PDF looks blurry

The source image may be low resolution, or the quality slider may be set too low. Use sharper source images and raise the quality setting. Avoid zooming into a small image before saving it.

The download does not start

Check your browser's download or pop-up permissions. Look for a blocked-download icon in the address bar. Try another browser or temporarily disable strict download blocking, then export again.

Pages are rotated or out of order

Use the page controls to rotate individual pages and drag cards to reorder them before export. Use the sort and reverse buttons for quick bulk ordering.

The watermark or header overlaps the image

Watermarks are drawn behind the image by design, so they remain visible in blank areas. If the image fills the entire page, the watermark may be hidden. Try adding margins or reducing the image scale.
